Understanding Bankruptcy

Bankruptcy is a legal process that provides relief to individuals who cannot repay their debts. It’s designed to give people a fresh start by either eliminating or restructuring their debts. The most common types of bankruptcy for individuals are Chapter 7 and Chapter 13.

Chapter 7 Bankruptcy, also known as liquidation bankruptcy, involves selling non-exempt assets to pay off creditors. It’s typically quicker but might require you to sell your home if it’s not exempt. Chapter 13 Bankruptcy allows you to keep your property while repaying debts over a 3-5 year period through a court-approved repayment plan.

How Bankruptcy Affects Your Home

The impact of bankruptcy on your home depends on several factors, including the type of bankruptcy you file, the amount of equity in your home, and Tennessee's exemption laws.

If your home’s equity is below the state’s exemption limit, you may be able to keep your home in a Chapter 7 bankruptcy. In a Chapter 13 bankruptcy, you can keep your home as long as you include mortgage payments in your repayment plan and stay current on them. Filing for bankruptcy triggers an automatic stay, which temporarily halts foreclosure proceedings and other collection activities.

Steps to Take When Facing Bankruptcy

Consulting with a bankruptcy attorney is essential. An experienced attorney can help you understand your options, the bankruptcy process, and how it will affect your home. Assess your financial situation by evaluating your debts, income, and expenses to determine the best course of action. Gather all relevant financial documents, including mortgage statements, tax returns, and a list of assets and debts. Explore alternatives to bankruptcy, such as debt settlement, loan modification, or selling your home to pay off debts.
