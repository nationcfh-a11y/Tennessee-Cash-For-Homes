Can You Legally Sell a Rental Property with Tenants in Place?

Yes, you can sell a rental property in Tennessee even if it is currently occupied by tenants. However, the process and your legal responsibilities will vary depending on the type of lease in place. If the tenant is on a fixed-term lease, the new owner must honor the lease until it expires unless the lease contains an early termination clause. If the tenant is on a month-to-month lease, you can generally terminate the tenancy with proper notice. According to Tennessee law (Tenn. Code § 66-28-512), landlords must provide at least 30 days’ notice to terminate a month-to-month tenancy. If you’re planning to sell, you should review the lease terms carefully and consult a local real estate attorney if necessary. It’s also important to note that tenants have the right to “quiet enjoyment,” meaning you must provide reasonable notice—usually 24 hours—before showing the property to potential buyers.

Options for Selling a Rental with Problem Tenants

When selling a rental with problem tenants, you have a few key paths forward. One option is to wait until the lease ends and the tenant vacates the property before listing it. This can help you market the property more easily, but it may not be viable if the tenant is non-compliant or if you need to sell quickly. Another approach is to initiate the eviction process before listing. In Tennessee, eviction (known legally as an unlawful detainer action) requires giving the tenant written notice of the violation and providing a period to remedy the issue. If the tenant fails to comply, you can file for eviction in General Sessions Court.

However, this process can take weeks or months, especially if the tenant contests the eviction or delays court proceedings. If you're preparing to sell a rental with problematic tenants, clear communication is key.
