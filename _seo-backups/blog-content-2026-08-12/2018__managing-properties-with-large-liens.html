Understanding Liens

A lien is a legal claim against a property, typically used as collateral to secure a debt. Common types of liens include tax liens, which are placed by the government for unpaid property taxes or income taxes, mechanic’s liens placed by contractors or suppliers for unpaid work or materials, and judgment liens arising from court judgments against you for unpaid debts. Liens must be resolved before you can sell or refinance your property, as they give the lienholder a right to a portion of the sale proceeds.

Steps to Take When Dealing with Large Liens

Start by obtaining a title report to identify all liens against your property. Verify the validity and amounts of these liens to ensure there are no errors. Contact the lienholders to discuss your situation. In some cases, you may be able to negotiate a settlement or payment plan to reduce the lien amount. If your credit allows, refinancing your mortgage might provide the funds needed to pay off the liens. This option depends on the amount of equity in your home and your ability to qualify for a new loan. Selling your property can also be a viable solution to resolve large liens, although this process can be complicated if the liens exceed the property’s value. Working with a professional can help navigate these complexities.
