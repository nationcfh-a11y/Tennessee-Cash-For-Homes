Do Not Mention That the Home Has Been on the Market a Long Time

If a buyer asks how long your home has been listed, be cautious about how you respond. Stating that the home has been sitting on the market for an extended period can immediately trigger concerns. Buyers may assume something is wrong with the property or that you are desperate to sell. Instead of focusing on time, redirect the conversation toward the home’s value, upgrades, or the attention it has received. Do Not Say You Are Not in a Hurry to Sell

Telling a potential buyer that you are not in a rush may seem like a way to project confidence, but it can actually backfire. It might signal that negotiations will be tough or that there is no urgency to move forward. This can cause serious buyers to delay their decisions or walk away. Avoid Talking About Outgrowing the House

Saying that you have outgrown the house implies that it might be too small, poorly designed, or not suitable for modern needs. While it may be true for your family, it may not be the case for every buyer. Comments like these can cause potential buyers to question whether the home will meet their own needs. A better approach is to highlight how the home functioned well for your lifestyle and allow buyers to imagine how it could work for theirs.
