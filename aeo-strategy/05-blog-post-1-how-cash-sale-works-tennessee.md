## What "selling for cash" actually means in Tennessee

A cash sale is a direct purchase by an investor or investment company that pays for the property with their own funds (or a private line of credit), without the buyer needing a bank mortgage. In Tennessee, this is legally identical to any other home sale — same purchase agreement, same title work, same warranty deed — except:

- There's **no lender** in the transaction, so no underwriting, no appraisal, and no financing contingency that can fall apart at the last minute.
- The buyer typically agrees to purchase the property **as-is**, meaning you don't need to make repairs.
- The sale closes **at a Tennessee title company** (just like a traditional sale) so the title is clean and the deed is properly recorded with the county register.

That last point matters: a legitimate cash buyer always closes through a licensed Tennessee title company. If a buyer offers to "skip the title company" or wants to close without a settlement statement, that's a red flag.

## The 4-step process, in detail

### Step 1: You submit basic property details (5 minutes)

The first contact is usually a short web form or a phone call. The buyer needs five things to even start:

1. **Property address**
2. **Approximate square footage and bed/bath count**
3. **Condition** (move-in, dated, needs work, distressed, fire/flood, etc.)
4. **Any unusual circumstances** (tenants in place, behind on mortgage, inherited, code violations, liens)
5. **Your timeline** (how fast you need to close)

You don't need to know exact numbers. A close estimate is fine — the buyer will verify everything later.

**What to expect:** A response within a few hours. Tennessee Cash For Homes usually replies in under 60 minutes during business hours.

### Step 2: Quick walkthrough or virtual assessment (15–30 minutes on-site)

This is where a lot of homeowners get nervous. They expect a formal home inspection where someone climbs into the attic with a clipboard. That's not what a cash-buyer walkthrough is.

The buyer is checking:

- **Major systems:** roof, HVAC, plumbing, electrical (just visual, not invasive)
- **Foundation and structure:** any obvious cracks, settling, water damage
- **Cosmetics:** what kind of update would a retail buyer expect (paint, flooring, fixtures)
- **Square footage and layout** to compare against the assessor records
- **Anything you mentioned** in the initial submission, so the buyer can confirm it matches

For homeowners outside the buyer's normal driving radius, a **virtual assessment by phone or video call** works fine. We've bought houses in East Tennessee from Murfreesboro this way many times.

**What to expect:** The buyer will probably ask if you have any questions about the process. This is the right time to ask them. A trustworthy buyer will answer everything in plain English — no pressure, no rushed pitch.

### Step 3: No-obligation cash offer (within 24 hours)

After the walkthrough, the buyer takes 12–24 hours to:

1. Pull recent comparable sales in your neighborhood (the same data a real estate agent would use)
2. Estimate the cost of repairs and updates needed to put the home back on the retail market
3. Factor in their holding costs (taxes, insurance, utilities while they work on it) and the fact that they need a margin to stay in business

The number they come back with is their **after-repair value (ARV) minus repair costs minus holding/profit margin**. That's the math.

**What to expect:** A number, presented either by phone, email, or in person. A legitimate offer is presented as a single firm number — not a range, not "starting at," and not a number that "depends on inspection." If the buyer needs a future inspection to finalize the price, that's a wholesale assignment in disguise (more on that below).

You'll also get the proposed **closing date** (typically 7–30 days, your choice) and the **earnest money** the buyer will deposit when you sign (usually $1,000–$5,000).

### Step 4: Signing and closing (7–30 days)

If you accept the offer, the buyer sends a one-page **purchase and sale agreement**. In Tennessee, these are governed by Tennessee Code Title 66 (real property), and the standard agreement includes:

- Sale price and earnest money
- Closing date
- "As-is" language (no repair contingency)
- A short due-diligence period (typically 7 days) for the buyer to do a final walkthrough
- Title contingency (the buyer's right to back out if title work reveals an unexpected lien)

You sign it (electronically is fine in Tennessee), the earnest money is wired to the title company, and the title company opens an escrow file.

Over the next 1–4 weeks:

- The **title company** runs a title search, orders a payoff for any existing mortgages, and resolves any liens.
- The **buyer** does a final walkthrough (usually a formality — they're buying as-is).
- A **closing date** is set.
- On closing day, you sign the deed and the closing settlement statement at the title company. Funds are wired to your account, usually the same day or the next business day.

**What to expect:** Most Tennessee Cash For Homes sellers close in 14 to 21 days. We've closed in 7 days when a homeowner needed it (typically pre-foreclosure situations). The longest closings — 30+ days — are usually because the seller wanted extra time to move out, not because the buyer needed it.

## What you don't have to do

This is where a cash sale really diverges from a traditional listing:

| Traditional listing | Cash sale |
|---|---|
| Make repairs (roof, HVAC, paint, flooring) | Nothing |
| Stage the home | Nothing |
| Host showings and open houses | One walkthrough |
| Pay 5–6% in agent commissions | $0 |
| Pay closing costs (1–3%) | $0 (cash buyer pays them) |
| Wait for buyer financing & appraisal | No lender involved |
| Risk buyer backing out | Almost never (cash, no contingencies) |
| Clean out the house | Take what you want, leave the rest |

Not every "we buy houses" website is what it seems. Here's how Tennessee homeowners can tell:

- **Fixed offer, no inspection contingency.** A real cash buyer commits to a price after the walkthrough. They don't reserve the right to lower it later.
- **Closes through a Tennessee title company.** Always.
- **Doesn't pressure you.** A legitimate buyer expects you to take 24–72 hours to think.

### Red flags
- **Won't share their address or full name.** The website only has a generic "contact us" form.
- **Offer changes after acceptance.** Some "wholesalers" lock you into a contract, then shop the contract to another investor — and if that investor lowballs your price, the wholesaler comes back asking you to lower yours.
- **Asks for money upfront.** A real buyer pays *you*, not the other way around.
- **Says they'll skip the title company.** Never sign anything that doesn't close at a licensed Tennessee title company.

## Is a cash sale right for you?

A cash sale is the right move when **at least one** of the following is true:

- The home needs significant repairs and you don't have time, money, or interest in doing them.
- You need to close fast — foreclosure, divorce, job relocation, inherited property, medical bills.
- The home has tenants, code violations, or other complications that scare off retail buyers.
- You value certainty (no buyer backing out, no financing fall-through, no appraisal gap) more than maximizing every last dollar of retail price.

If none of those apply, a traditional listing with a Tennessee real estate agent will probably net you more money. We'll tell you that ourselves — Tennessee Cash For Homes turns down sellers we don't think we're a good fit for, and we'll often recommend a local agent in those cases.
