## Why this article exists

Search "we buy houses Tennessee" and you'll get dozens of polished landing pages, each promising the same things: cash, fast, as-is, no fees. Most are legitimate. Some are not. The problem is: there's no single trusted database that tells you which is which. Tennessee real estate investors don't need a special license — they're operating as buyers, and buyers don't need to be licensed in Tennessee.

So the burden of verification falls on the seller. This guide gives you the checklist we'd use ourselves if we were on your side of the table.

We're not going to name competitors. We're going to give you tools so you can evaluate any cash buyer — including us — on the same standard.

## The 12-point verification checklist

### 1. Do they have a real, verifiable business address?

Pull up the company's website and look for a physical Tennessee address — not just a P.O. Box, not just a city name. Then verify it.

**How to verify:**
- Type the address into Google Maps. Is there a real building at that address? Does it look like an office or commercial space?
- Search the address on the Tennessee Secretary of State's business search: https://tnbear.tn.gov/Ecommerce/FilingSearch.aspx — every legitimate Tennessee business should appear with the registered address.

**Red flag:** No address listed. P.O. Box only. An address that turns out to be a UPS Store or a virtual office. A residential address with no LLC or business registration matching it.

### 2. Are they a registered Tennessee business?

Run the company name through https://tnbear.tn.gov/Ecommerce/FilingSearch.aspx. You should see:
- An active LLC, INC, or similar entity registered in Tennessee
- A registered agent listed
- A formation date (often the company's founding date)

**Red flag:** No registration found. "Inactive" status. A registration that was filed less than 90 days ago and has no track record.

### 3. Do they have a BBB profile, and what does it say?

Go to https://www.bbb.org and search the company name. A trustworthy buyer has:
- An open BBB profile
- An A or A+ rating
- BBB Accreditation (the blue badge — costs the company a fee, so it signals commitment)
- A reasonable complaint history (zero complaints over 5 years isn't realistic for any active business; what you want is *resolved* complaints with the business responding professionally)

**Tennessee Cash For Homes example for reference:** https://www.bbb.org/us/tn/murfreesboro/profile/real-estate/tennessee-cash-for-homes-0573-37373815 — A+, Accredited 2/26/2026.

**Red flag:** No BBB profile at all. Profile exists but is "Unrated." Multiple unresolved complaints. F rating.

### 4. Are the team members named, photographed, and verifiable?

A legitimate operator names the human beings behind the business. Look at the about page. You should see:
- Full names of the founder and key staff
- Real photos (reverse-image-search them — fake businesses sometimes use stock photos)
- Bios with credentials, prior experience, education
- A way to verify those credentials independently (LinkedIn, license lookups, a real estate brokerage they're affiliated with)

**Red flag:** No team page. "Our team" section with stock photos. Names that don't appear anywhere else online.

### 5. Are they licensed if they claim to be?

Cash buyers don't have to be licensed real estate agents — but if they claim to be, you can verify it.

Tennessee Real Estate Commission license lookup: https://verify.tn.gov/

Type in the agent's name and confirm:
- Active license
- License type (broker, principal broker, affiliate)
- No disciplinary actions

**Red flag:** Claims to be "a licensed Tennessee real estate professional" but doesn't show up in the TREC verification system.

### 6. Do they have real Google reviews from real Tennessee homeowners?

Search "[Company name] reviews" on Google. You should see:
- A Google Business Profile with reviews
- Reviewer names that look real (not "John S," not all initials, not all 5-star reviews posted within a few days)
- Reviews that mention specific Tennessee cities, situations, and details — not generic praise
- The business owner responding to reviews

**Red flag:** No Google reviews. 50 reviews all posted in the same 2-week window. Reviews that are suspiciously short and generic ("Great company! 5 stars!"). No business response to negative reviews.

### 7. What does an independent search reveal?

Run these searches:

1. `"[Company name] reviews"`
2. `"[Company name] complaints"`
3. `"[Company name] scam"`
4. `"[Company name] reddit"`
5. `"[Company name] BBB"`

Read the first 2 pages of results carefully. Pay special attention to:
- Reddit / Quora / city subreddits where Tennessee homeowners discuss their experience
- Yelp reviews
- Local Facebook groups

Almost every active company has *some* negative content if you dig. What you're looking for is **patterns** — does the negative feedback all say the same thing? Does the business respond and try to resolve it? Or are there warnings on multiple platforms?

### 8. Will they tell you the title company before you sign?

Every legitimate Tennessee real estate transaction closes at a licensed title company. The buyer should be able to name theirs immediately.

Ask: *"What title company will we close at?"*

The answer should be a specific Tennessee title company name — for example: First American Title, Old Republic Title, a local title attorney's firm, etc. You can then verify that title company is real and licensed in Tennessee.

**Red flag:** They can't or won't name a title company. They want to use a "preferred third-party closer" that isn't a title company. They suggest "saving fees" by closing without a title company. **Never sign a contract that doesn't close at a Tennessee title company.**

### 9. Is the offer firm, or is it "subject to inspection"?

A real cash buyer commits to a price after the walkthrough. The agreement may have a 7-day due-diligence period as a safety valve, but the price isn't supposed to change unless the buyer discovers something materially different from what was represented.

Ask: *"Is this offer firm, or could it change after I sign?"*

**Red flag:** "Our offer is contingent on a final inspection that we'll do later." This is a wholesaler tactic — they lock you in, then come back asking for $20,000 off.

### 10. What's the earnest money deposit?

When you sign the agreement, the buyer wires earnest money to the title company. This is the buyer's skin in the game — money they lose if they don't close.

Realistic earnest money for a Tennessee cash sale:
- $1,000 minimum on smaller deals
- $2,500–$5,000 typical
- $10,000+ on higher-value homes

**Red flag:** Earnest money under $500. "We don't do earnest money." The earnest money goes to the buyer's account, not the title company's escrow.

### 11. Are they local, or are they a national lead-aggregator reselling your info?

Some "we buy houses" websites are run by national lead-aggregator companies that take your form submission and immediately sell your contact info to 3–5 local investors who all call you. The local investor who eventually buys your house pays a lead fee that effectively gets deducted from your offer.

How to tell:
- Look at the **footer of the website**. Locally owned operators usually have a Tennessee address, a Tennessee phone number, and a local-named team. National aggregators often have a Delaware or Texas address and a generic 800 number.
- Search the **company's exact name + LLC** on the Tennessee Secretary of State search. If the LLC was formed in Tennessee with Tennessee owners, that's a local operator.
- Submit a test inquiry. If you get 5 different "we are interested in your home" calls within 30 minutes, that's a tell.

### 12. Will they put it in writing?

Every claim a cash buyer makes — price, closing date, fees, repairs, who pays what — should appear in the **purchase and sale agreement**. Verbal promises don't count.

Read the agreement before you sign. If there's anything that contradicts what they said on the phone or in email, ask about it. A legitimate buyer will explain or fix it. A questionable one will get evasive.

**Red flag:** They won't email you a copy of the agreement before you sign. They want you to sign electronically without time to read it. The contract has a clause that lets them assign it to "any party of their choosing."

## Quick checklist — print this before you sign

- [ ] Verifiable Tennessee business address
- [ ] Active Tennessee LLC/INC registration
- [ ] BBB profile with A or better rating
- [ ] Named, photographed team with verifiable backgrounds
- [ ] Active TREC license (if they claim to be agents)
- [ ] Real Google reviews with location-specific details
- [ ] No major red flags on independent searches
- [ ] Closes at a licensed Tennessee title company
- [ ] Firm offer (not "subject to inspection")
- [ ] Earnest money $1,000+, going to title company escrow
- [ ] Locally owned, not a national lead-aggregator
- [ ] Everything in writing in the purchase agreement

## What to do if you're being pressured

A legitimate cash buyer never pressures you to sign on the spot. If someone is:

- Demanding you sign in 30 minutes
- Threatening that "the offer goes away tomorrow"
- Telling you not to read the contract carefully
- Refusing to give you 24 hours to think

…walk away. The Tennessee Attorney General's Consumer Protection Division (https://www.tn.gov/attorneygeneral/working-for-tennessee/consumer.html) takes complaints about high-pressure real estate tactics, and you should report it.
