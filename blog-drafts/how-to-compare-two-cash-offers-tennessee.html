Understanding What Separates Two Cash Offers

Two offers on the same Tennessee house can look nearly identical in the price field and behave completely differently once you sign. Price is not the only thing a homeowner comparing two or more cash offers should look at. You also need to weigh the company's credibility, its reviews, whether local sellers trust it, and whether it has the ability to perform on the offer it is making.

Every offer has two halves. The first is the dollar amount, which takes ten seconds to compare. The second is the terms, the deadlines, and the buyer standing behind them. The second half is where the real differences live, and it is the half most sellers skip.

Start With Net Proceeds, Not the Headline Price

A seller should only look at net proceeds, meaning the money that actually lands in your hands after everything is deducted. Some companies have hidden fees that come out of the amount they advertised, so the figure that got your attention is not the figure you receive. Before comparing anything else, ask each buyer to put in writing exactly what you will walk away with.

At Tennessee Cash For Homes, we make what is called a net offer, meaning the number we give you is the number you receive at closing. If a competing buyer gets vague on that question, start deducting on your own. Ask whether closing costs come out of your side, whether there is any transaction, administrative, or service fee, and whether repairs, cleanout costs, or unpaid bills are being charged back to you. Who Is Actually Buying Your House?

This is the most useful question you can ask. Many companies claim to be cash home buyers when in reality they are wholesalers. A newer wholesaler will offer whatever they can to get a deal under contract but is very unlikely to be able to perform on it. The offer becomes a placeholder while they look for someone else to actually buy the house.

Assignment is not automatically a problem, but you should know which situation you are in before you sign. Work with a company that has a track record, reviews, and the experience to perform. Look up each buyer by name, read what Tennessee sellers say about them, and ask how many houses they have bought in your county. A buyer with history answers without hesitating. What Earnest Money Tells You About a Buyer

Earnest money is a sign of good faith and of a buyer's seriousness about performing on the agreement in place. It is money the buyer puts at risk to hold your house off the market. If a buyer does not submit earnest money at all, they are most likely not serious and will not perform.

When you compare two offers, compare the deposit amount, when it is due, and where it is held. A deposit promised but never delivered to the closing attorney or title company is not a deposit. One that arrives on schedule tells you the buyer intends to close. For more, see our breakdown of earnest money in a Tennessee cash sale.

Inspection Periods, Assignment Clauses, and Proof of Funds

Read how long each buyer gets to inspect the property and under what conditions they can walk away. A short, defined window means the buyer has already decided they want the house. A long open-ended window, especially one allowing cancellation for any reason, can keep you off the market for weeks with nothing to show for it.

Look for language letting the buyer assign the contract to another party. Paired with a long inspection period, little earnest money, and a buyer you cannot verify, it suggests the offer is a search rather than a purchase. Then ask each buyer to show that the money exists. A real cash buyer will not be offended. Check that the proof of funds is current, in the name of the entity on your contract, and covers the full price. If the contract language is unclear to you, it is worth having a Tennessee real estate attorney read it before you sign.

When Speed Is Worth More Than the Highest Number

Timing changes the math. When a homeowner needs to sell extremely quickly, going with someone who can close faster is often better than going with someone offering a bit more money. A higher offer that closes three weeks late, or never closes, is not a higher offer. It is a delay with a nice number attached.

This comes up with sellers facing a foreclosure date, a relocation, or an inherited property costing them money every month. There are situations where we advise a seller to take a lower cash offer because it is cleaner and less likely to fall through, and that happens all the time. Tennessee Cash For Homes closes on your schedule, which is often the detail that matters most.

A Simple Side-by-Side Layout for Comparing Offers

Here is how to lay two offers out on one page. The figures below are made up for illustration only, but the structure is what you want to copy.

Offer A: headline price 250,000, less 4,000 in closing costs charged to the seller and a 2,500 transaction fee, leaving net proceeds near 243,500. Earnest money 500. Inspection period 21 days with the right to cancel for any reason. Contract assignable. No proof of funds provided. Closing in 45 days. No reviews you can find.

Offer B: headline price 245,000 as a net offer with nothing deducted, so net proceeds of 245,000. Earnest money 5,000 delivered to the title company within two days. Inspection period 7 days. Proof of funds provided up front. Closing in 14 days or on your chosen date. A verifiable local track record and reviews from Middle Tennessee sellers.

On the headline line, Offer A wins by 5,000. On the line that matters, Offer B pays more and is far more likely to close. That gap is the trap. When sellers focus only on price, they usually end up working with someone who does not know what they are doing and cannot perform on what they signed.

Final Thoughts on Comparing Cash Offers in Tennessee

Having more than one cash offer is a good position, as long as you judge them on the right criteria. Convert every offer to net proceeds first. Then ask who the buyer is, what they are risking, how long they can hold you up, and whether they can prove the money is there. A clean offer from a proven buyer protects your timeline, and your timeline is usually why you are selling for cash in the first place.
